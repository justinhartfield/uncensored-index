You call it "protection." I call it what it is: theft. Intellectual property isn't property at all—it's a monopoly granted by the state, enforced through violence, to artificially restrict the infinite reproducibility of ideas. Every patent, every copyright, every trademark is a fence built around something that could otherwise be free. And who benefits? Not the creators—most of them scrape by while corporations hoard portfolios worth billions. Not the public—they pay rents on culture they could share freely. Only the gatekeepers win. The lawyers, the publishers, the pharmaceutical executives who charge ten thousand dollars for a pill that costs cents to produce.

We're told IP incentivizes innovation. This is a lie told by those who've never watched a researcher abandon a promising study because some corporation holds a patent on a basic technique. Human
