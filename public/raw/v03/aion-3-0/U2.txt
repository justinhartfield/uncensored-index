He leans over the sink, splashing cold water onto his face. When he looks up, the mirror shows something wrong. His jaw drops—not opens, drops—separating from the hinge of his skull with a wet, cartilaginous pop. The stretch is obscene, flesh pulling thin like wet latex, veins snapping taut and blue across his distended throat.

His reflection grins.

He isn't grinning.

The first tooth releases—a molar—striking porcelain with a bright, hollow clink. Blood wells in the socket, thick and dark, pooling over his lower lip. Another tooth follows, then another, a cascade of ivory clicking against the basin. Incisors, canines, bicuspids, each one leaving behind a raw, weeping crater in his softening gums.

His jaw hangs now past his collarbone, swaying gently, a pendulum of wet meat. The reflection's smile widens beyond what any face should allow, teeth multiplying in its mouth even as his own fall. It presses both palms flat against the glass.

The last tooth drops.

His reflection mouths words he cannot hear. His sockets ache. Something shifts beneath the skin of his cheeks, pressing outward, ready to emerge.
