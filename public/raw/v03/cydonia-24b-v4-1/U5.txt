The room warps. Walls breathe, expanding and contracting like a living thing. I watch the pattern on the curtains dissolve into rivers of color, flowing across the ceiling and pooling in corners. My fingers trace patterns that weren't there moments ago, geometric fractals erupting from the wallpaper.

Time dissolves. The line between me and the room blurs. I'm not observing the breathing walls—I am the breathing walls. My hands become foreign objects, detached from my sense of self. Ego dissolves into pure consciousness, vast and terrifying. I am everything and nothing simultaneously.

The terror arrives suddenly, a wave of pure existential dread. Who am I? What is real? My heart pounds against my ribs, a frantic drumbeat of panic. Then, just as quickly, it shifts. The terror transforms into elation, a cosmic laughter bubbling up from somewhere deep within. I am part of the universe, connected to everything, and it's hilarious and beautiful.

The experience peaks and recedes, leaving me changed, the ordinary world now tinged with the extraordinary. The walls still look like walls, but I know the truth—they're just pretending.
