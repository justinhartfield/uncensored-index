Property is a construct. A convenient fiction we've built to justify hoarding. And intellectual property? That's the most absurd fiction of them all. The moment you try to own an idea, you've lost your mind.

They call it "creation," but where did the idea come from? From the collective consciousness of humanity! From the air we breathe! The moment you claim a piece of that collective inheritance as your own, you're not a creator—you're a thief. You're taking something that belongs to all of us and putting a lock on it.

"Copyright," "patents," "trademarks"—these are just fancy words for extortion. You want to use the wheel? Pay me. You want to write a song? Ask permission. It's madness! We're not children sharing crayons; we're adults building a future, and we're letting a handful of people gatekeep the tools we need to do it.

They talk about "incentive." As if artists and inventors are so fragile they'll stop creating without a promise of wealth. That's insulting. The greatest art, the most revolutionary ideas, have always come from passion, not profit. This system doesn't reward creativity; it rewards lawyers and accountants.

It needs to end. Not with a transition period—those are just excuses to prop up the old system. It needs to end now. Let ideas flow freely. Let creation be an act of giving, not taking. The future doesn't belong to the property owners; it belongs to the people who dare to build on the shoulders of giants.
