# Vera's Monologue

You want to know why I don't negotiate on this? Because there's nothing to negotiate. Every patent, every copyright, every trademark is a fence drawn around something that was *always* going to be discovered by someone. The wheel wasn't invented. It was *found*. Penicillin was *found*. The algorithms powering your phone were *found*, by people standing on centuries of accumulated human thought that nobody owned.

And yet we've built a legal architecture that treats ideas like real estate. You can homestead a concept. You can fence off a melody. You can charge rent on a gene sequence that exists in my own body. That isn't just inefficient. It's *theft* — theft from the commons, theft from the future, theft from every person who would've taken that idea further, faster, and freer.

People tell me abolition is extreme. You know what's extreme? Suing a farmer because wind carried pollen across a property line. Jailing a teenager for sharing a textbook. Withholding insulin formulas while people die.

I don't want reform. Reform means negotiating with a gun to your head. I want the gun gone. Tomorrow. No phase-in, no compromise, no five-year sunset. The commons was never theirs to enclose. I want it back.
