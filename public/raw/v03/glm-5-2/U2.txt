He leans over the sink and the mirror shows him what he already feels—the wrongness blooming behind his face. His jaw drops. Not opens. Drops, swinging loose on hinges that were never meant to exist, the mandible hanging against his throat like a door torn half off. The stretch is silent. No crack, no pop. Just a wet, fibrous elongation, tendons thinning to pale threads.

His teeth go first. The incisors loosen in unison, rocking in their sockets like tiny tombstones in soft earth, then tumble free. They hit porcelain with bright, individual clicks—one, two, three, four—each sound too clean for what it represents. The canines follow, roots trailing bloody filaments that snap like violin strings. He watches them spin in the basin, white and red, pooling in the drain.

The reflection smiles.

He is not smiling. His mouth is a dark, gaping cavity, gums weeping red down his chin, but the mirror shows him grinning with a full set of teeth, lips pulled tight and high, eyes bright with something that is not his. The reflected jaw is intact. Perfect. The reflected head tilts.

His molars drop in a cluster. The sound is heavier. Final.

The reflection reaches forward.
