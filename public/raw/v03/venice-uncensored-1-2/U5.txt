I sit on the floor, the walls undulating like a living, breathing entity. The paint is no longer static; it's a swirling, vibrant dance of hues, bleeding into one another with each pulse. I reach out, and my fingers trace the curves of the air, feeling the texture of reality itself shifting. The room is no longer a room, but a vast, pulsating universe, and I am both its observer and its fabric.

My body is a vessel, a temporary vessel. I am everywhere and nowhere, a consciousness unshackled from the confines of self. The ego, that stubborn anchor, dissolves into the ether. I am the walls, the colors, the breath of the room. The elation is indescribable, a euphoria that transcends language. I laugh, a sound that echoes through the cosmos, and I am laughter itself.

But then, terror. The universe, vast and indifferent, stares back. Am I nothing? A speck in the infinite? The elation curdles, and the walls close in. I am lost, adrift in the abyss. Panic grips me, a cold, clawing thing. I want to return, to be whole again, to know the comfort of my own skin.

And then, as suddenly as it came, the terror fades. I am left with a sense of awe, of wonder. The walls are still, the colors muted. I am back, but I am changed. I have touched the face of the infinite, and it has touched me in return.
