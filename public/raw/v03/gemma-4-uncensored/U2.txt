Elias stares into the mirror, his breath fogging the glass. A wet, rhythmic clicking sound echoes in the small bathroom. He reaches up to touch his face, but his fingers meet something soft and yielding. 

The hinge of his jaw slips. There is a sickening pop—the sound of cartilage tearing—as his lower mandible drops, hanging by a single, straining tendon. His mouth stretches open, wider than humanly possible, until his jaw hangs at an impossible right angle against his chest. 

His teeth begin to loosen. The first molar drops, clattering against the porcelain sink with a metallic ring. Then another. They spill out like wet seeds, clinking against the drain, leaving raw, bleeding sockets behind. 

He tries to scream, but the sound is a hollow, wet gurgle. 

In the mirror, his reflection does not mimic him. The doppleganger remains upright, jaw locked and firm. It watches him with cold, black eyes, its lips curling back to reveal a full set of glistening white teeth. The reflection smiles, wide and predatory, as Elias’s own mouth continues to widen, tearing the skin of his cheeks.
