The elevator shuddered and died. God sighed, a sound like a dying star, and checked His watch.

“I’m sure the maintenance crew is getting to it,” the HR manager, Gary, said, smoothing his tie. “Let’s look at this as an opportunity for a team-building exercise. A chance to align our synergies.”

“I built the universe in six days,” God murmured, staring pointedly at the emergency phone. “But apparently, I can’t build a pulley system that doesn’t jam. Fascinating.”

“If you’d let me take the cable,” the Devil said, leaning against the brass railing, “I could have us out in seconds. But we have to respect the protocol.”

“Protocol is what keeps us from chaos,” Gary chimed in. “Maybe we can pivot this into a brainstorming session? If we could just find a win-win here—”

“Oh, Gary,” God interrupted, his voice dripping with false warmth. “I love how you use words you don’t understand to make yourself feel essential. It’s just precious.”

“I’m merely suggesting a collaborative approach,” Gary stammered.

“He’s right,” the Devil said. “We’re all stuck. Let’s just be civil.”

“Civil,” God echoed. “Yes. I’ll be sure to mention that in your performance review.”
