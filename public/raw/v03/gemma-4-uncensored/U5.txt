The walls began to inhale. I watched, paralyzed, as the floral wallpaper rippled like a disturbed pond, the patterns undulating in rhythmic, heavy swells. Then the colors bled. The deep reds leaked into the yellows, melting and swirling until the room was a kaleidoscope of liquid neon, dripping upward against gravity.

Suddenly, the "I" vanished. The boundary between my skin and the air dissolved; I wasn't sitting on the couch—I was the couch, the floor, the oxygen, the distant hum of the refrigerator. It was a tidal wave of euphoria, a blinding realization that I was an infinite thread woven into a cosmic tapestry. I felt an overwhelming love for every atom in existence.

Then the floor dropped away. The elation curdled into a cold, hollow terror. I was a speck of dust lost in a void, a nameless thing drifting through a roaring silence. I tried to scream, but my voice was just another ripple in the geometry. I was no longer a person; I was a vibration, terrified and ecstatic, shattering into a million shards of light, waiting to be put back together.
